# MVP quality bar — beat the generic video baseline

## Audience and job

The primary user is a podcast creator who wants to transform an episode, transcript, document, or research bundle into a distinctive video while preserving their voice, identity, and editorial intent.

## Product gate

The MVP earns its place when target creators prefer its output over same-source results from Gemini Notebook and Google Vids. The comparison uses identical source material, audience, duration, language, and aspect ratio.

## Prerequisites

- import audio, transcript, document, URL, and images;
- create or import a consented avatar;
- record, import, or connect the creator’s voice;
- edit scenes, script, captions, framing, timing, and media;
- render 16:9 and 9:16;
- preserve source references and generation provenance;
- revise one scene without rebuilding the whole project;
- export video, captions, thumbnail, manifest, and QA report.

## Comparative scorecard

| Dimension | MVP gate |
|---|---:|
| Pairwise preference against each baseline | at least 60% across target creators |
| Supported claims linked to source evidence | at least 95% |
| Required story points represented | at least 90% |
| Critical unsupported claims | 0 |
| Voice/avatar consent receipts | 100% |
| Approval bypasses | 0 |
| Duplicate renders for one idempotency key | 0 |
| Caption accuracy from an approved transcript | at least 98% |
| Scene-level revision without full rebuild | 100% of tested projects |
| Final output profiles | 1080p 16:9 and 9:16 |

## Human evaluation rubric

Five podcast creators rate each blinded result from one to five on:

1. fidelity to the episode;
2. visual identity;
3. pacing and narrative clarity;
4. voice quality;
5. avatar credibility and synchronization;
6. editability;
7. readiness to publish.

The test set contains at least five episodes: interview, solo explanation, technical discussion, story-driven episode, and bilingual episode.

## Competitive distinction

Google Vids offers strong collaborative scene editing, templates, recording, stock media, AI voiceovers, avatars, and Workspace integration. Gemini Notebook offers fast source-grounded summaries and audio/video overviews. Microsoft Clipchamp provides the Microsoft editing benchmark with timeline control, recording, captions, voiceovers, multiple aspect ratios, and Copilot-assisted drafts. OpenAI Sora provides a generative-scene benchmark. QuaNTecH-ViD differentiates through creator identity, deep source fidelity, reusable art direction, provider choice, local rendering, traceable approvals, and repeatable QA.

## Safety and identity

Avatar and voice workflows collect explicit consent, allowed uses, provider, source samples, creation time, disclosure, revocation state, and deletion receipt. A creator can switch providers while keeping the same project and editorial structure.
